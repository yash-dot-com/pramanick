# Pramanik — Community office edition

113 seconds · 1280 × 720 · 30 fps · silent.

The community, rather than an individual account, is the subject of the film. Residents report shared issues, an office of specialist voice agents pursues responsible officers and local leaders, and everyone can inspect the follow-through.

## Exact on-screen narrative

- 0–3s: India has a follow-up problem.
- 3–6s: Communities pay taxes. Public servants owe them answers.
- 6–13s: Residents report the same problems. Repairs still need chasing.
- 13–17s: A community cannot spend its days calling offices for basic services.
- 17–21s: Introducing Pramanik.
- 21–25s: Many residents. One public office. Agents do the follow-through.
- 25–32s: Agents combine residents’ complaints into shared cases for the community.
- 32–36s: Each case keeps the community’s reports, evidence, conversations and promises.
- 36–44s: The office splits work between agents: reports, memory and responsibility.
- 44–48s: Agents find responsible officers and leaders in public ward directories.
- 48–55s: Voice agents call officers and local leaders in Hindi, Marathi or English.
- 55–59s: Agents remember what officers promised and ask what has actually changed.
- 59–65s: Agents save each deadline and make the next follow-up call.
- 65–72s: Officers report a repair. Agents ask affected residents to check.
- 72–80s: Residents verify the work together, using surveys, photos and observations.
- 80–84s: Agents compare residents’ evidence with the officer’s completion claim.
- 84–89s: If evidence conflicts, the case reopens. Agents call again for answers and action.
- 89–96s: One public dashboard tracks all the community’s cases.
- 96–106s: Everyone can inspect each call, promise, follow-up and ground check.
- 106–110s: Your community raises the issues. Pramanik’s agents pursue the answers.
- 110–113s: Pramanik.

The introduction also reads “Your community’s accountability office, staffed by autonomous voice agents.” The final brand frame reads “Your community’s accountability office.”

## Motion and panels

- Seven resident avatars introduce the shared office. Five metallic agent avatars identify reports, evidence, coordination, calls and follow-ups.
- Dashboard, contact-directory and public-case panels are 640 × 480 (4:3). Resident verification cards are 510 × 382.5 (4:3).
- Directory and dashboard surfaces are sharp; the frosted header and scrolling-row blur are removed. Narrative Soft Blur In and the Fog Rise brand entrance remain.
- The community dashboard uses existing water, road and lighting case records from the local application. Report counts and statuses come from those records.
- The source is a presentational rendering styled with the project’s existing tokens. It does not place calls or submit observations.
